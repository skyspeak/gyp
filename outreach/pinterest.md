# Pinterest

Pinterest is the one channel here where the audience is mostly parents, and
where a pin keeps working for two years. It's a search engine, so the words in
the pin title and description do the work.

Pin graphics are in `pins.html`. Open it, screenshot each card at 1000x1500,
or print to PDF and export pages as images.

## Account setup

Business account, not personal. Name it `Gap Year Platform`. Bio:

```
Free index of 371 gap year programs, sorted by which ones pay you and which
ones charge you. No commissions, no sponsored listings.
```

Claim the website so pins get attribution and you can see analytics.

## Boards

Make these five. Pinterest ranks boards, so the board name is a keyword slot.

| Board | Description |
|---|---|
| Gap year programs that pay you | Stipends, wages and education awards for the year after high school or college. |
| Gap year cost comparison | What gap year programs actually cost, with the paid alternatives side by side. |
| Gap year after high school | Options for a deferred college start, including paid ones. |
| Gap year after college | Fellowships, conservation corps and paid work for new graduates. |
| Gap year application deadlines | When to apply, by month. |

## Pins

Ten pins. Post two a week, not all at once. Each one links to a page that
actually answers the title, which is what stops it being spam.

Descriptions are written for Pinterest search. They repeat the phrase people
type. That's deliberate, not padding.

## Posting rhythm

Two pins a week for five weeks, then repin the best performers to a second
relevant board each month. Pinterest rewards steady posting far more than
volume, and a pin often takes three months to start ranking. Don't judge any
of this before December.
