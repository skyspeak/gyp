# 3. Press pitch

Three angles. Send **one** per reporter. Every figure was checked against the
live catalog or the program's own site on 15 September 2026. Recheck anything
older than a month before sending.

Attach the one-page data sheet: open `03-data-sheet.html` in a browser, print
to PDF, attach.

## Who to pitch

Pitch beats, not names. Find the right reporter on each outlet's own staff
page, and read two of their recent stories before writing to them.

| Angle | Beat | Where it fits |
|---|---|---|
| A. Fellowships that ended are still advertised | Higher education | Inside Higher Ed, The Chronicle of Higher Education, The Hechinger Report |

## Angle A — fellowships that ended are still listed

Send now. Fellowship season is on.

**Subject:** `Federal fellowships that ended are still advertised on campus pages`

```
Hi {{first_name}},

I run a free, non-commercial index of gap year and post-grad programs, and
while checking it I found something that might be a story.

Several federally backed fellowships students plan careers around have
stopped, but university fellowships offices are still advertising them as
open:

- The Payne International Development Fellowship was terminated on
  27 February 2025 with the USAID shutdown. Its application site no longer
  exists.
- The Pickering and Rangel Foreign Affairs Fellowships both postponed their
  2026 cycles pending State Department guidance.
- The Mitchell Scholarship to Ireland has been paused since March 2024.

In the last week I found fellowship pages at six universities still
presenting one of these as open, some with application deadlines from 2023
and 2024. Operators rarely announce closures, so the pages don't get updated.

I can share the sources for each program and walk through how I checked. I'm
not naming the universities publicly; the point is the pattern, not
embarrassing individual offices.

Gautam
https://gyp-psi.vercel.app/?ref=press-{{outlet}}
```

## Angle B — what gap years cost

Best in December, when early decision results arrive and families start
talking about deferring.

**Subject:** `The $75,000 gap year, and the 230 that pay instead`

```
Hi {{first_name}},

Families researching gap years mostly find the programs with marketing
budgets, and those are the ones that charge. A postgraduate year at a
boarding school runs up to $75,000. EF Gap Year is around $43,750. Verto's
semester abroad is $33,500.

I track 371 gap year and post-grad programs, and 230 of the ones still
running pay the participant a stipend, wage or education award. In most
categories the paid programs outnumber the priced ones: in conservation,
7 programs charge and 40 pay. In teaching abroad, 5 charge and 29 pay.

Happy to send the full category breakdown and sources. Nothing on the site is
sponsored and it takes no commissions, which is why it can publish the
prices.

Gautam
https://gyp-psi.vercel.app/?ref=press-{{outlet}}
```

## Angle C — the exception

**Subject:** `The one kind of gap year you'll almost always pay for`

```
Hi {{first_name}},

I track gap year programs and sort them by whether they pay you or charge
you. Most categories lean heavily toward paying: conservation is 7 priced
programs against 40 that pay. Travel-and-study is mixed, 44 charge and 31
pay, but there are still plenty of paid options there.

Outdoor and wilderness programs are the exception: 15 charge and only 4
pay. If a student's dream year is a mountaineering or backcountry semester,
the family should budget for it, and the paid alternatives mostly don't
exist.

It's a small, counterintuitive finding, and I have the full breakdown
if it's useful.

Gautam
https://gyp-psi.vercel.app/?ref=press-{{outlet}}
```

## If a reporter asks which universities

Don't give names on the record. Offer to show the pattern with the pages
anonymized, and suggest they check a few university fellowship pages
themselves; it takes about ten minutes. You want these offices to see you as
help, and appearing as a named example in a news story would end that.
