# 15. College Advising Corps

College Advising Corps places recent college graduates as full-time college
advisers in underserved high schools, funded through AmeriCorps. Most advisers
are first-generation or Pell-eligible graduates themselves, and they serve for
two years.

That makes them a double audience:

1. **Their students**: first-generation and low-income seniors, the students
   a paid gap year would help most and a $40,000 one would hurt most.
2. **Themselves**: they're in a paid service year and will be deciding what
   comes next in a year or two.

## Who to write to

Write to the leadership of each state or partner-university program, not
individual advisers, who are busy with seniors. Find partner programs on
collegeadvisingcorps.org; each state program usually lists a director.

## The email

**Subject:** `Paid gap year options for your advisers' students`

```
Hi {{first_name}},

I run a free, non-commercial index of gap year and post-grad programs, and
I think it fits the students your advisers work with.

The gap year programs families hear about first are the expensive ones:
EF Gap Year is around $43,750, and some run higher. But there are 230
programs still running that pay the participant a stipend, wage or education
award: conservation corps, AmeriCorps positions, teaching programs abroad.
For a first-generation student considering a year before college, that's
the difference between a gap year being possible or not.

Nothing on the site is sponsored and it takes no commissions from anyone.

https://gyp-psi.vercel.app/?ref=cac-{{state}}

It may also be useful for your advisers themselves when they're weighing what
to do after their service years.

If a short resource sheet for your advisers would help, I'm happy to make
one.

Gautam
```
