# LinkedIn and X

LinkedIn matters here because fellowship advisers, college counselors and
study-abroad staff are all on it under their real names. It's the same
audience as the email campaign, reached sideways.

X matters less. Post there because it's cheap, not because it will work.

No hashtag stacks, no "thoughts?" closers, no thread-bait numbering.

## LinkedIn

Post one a week. Text only. Links in the post body cost reach on LinkedIn, so
where noted, put the link in the first comment instead.

**Post 1, the closures. Run this one first.**
```
Four fellowships that are still on university advising pages and are not
currently accepting applications:

Payne Fellowship. Terminated 27 February 2025 with the USAID shutdown.

Pickering and Rangel. Both postponed their 2026 cycles pending State
Department guidance, no restart announced.

Mitchell Scholarship. The US-Ireland Alliance paused selection of future
classes in March 2024. The notice is still on their own application page.

I found these while building a free index of gap year and post-grad programs.
Operators don't announce closures, so advising pages carry them for years, and
a student can spend a month on an application to something that ended.

If you advise students, worth a look at your own page before the October
deadlines.
```
Link in the first comment: `https://gyp-psi.vercel.app/?ref=li-closures`

**Post 2, the price comparison.**
```
A postgraduate year at a boarding school runs to $75,000. EF Gap Year is
around $43,750. Verto is $33,500.

Those are the gap year programs families find first, because those are the
ones with a marketing budget.

There are also 231 programs that pay a stipend, wage or education award for a
comparable year. Conservation is the clearest case: 8 programs charge, 39 pay.

I built a free index of 371 of them, sorted by that one question. No
commissions from any program, in either direction, which is the only reason it
can say what it says.
```
Link in the first comment: `https://gyp-psi.vercel.app/?ref=li-cost`

**Post 3, the exception. Post this third, on purpose.**
```
Something I'd rather publish than hide.

The argument behind the index I built is that most gap year categories have a
paid alternative to the ones families get sold. Conservation: 8 charge, 39
pay. Teaching abroad: 5 charge, 28 pay.

Outdoor and wilderness breaks the pattern. 15 charge, 4 pay. If that's the
year a student wants, they're most likely paying for it, and no amount of
sorting changes that.

Publishing the case where your own thesis fails is cheap and it's the reason
to believe the rest.
```
No link. This one is for credibility.
