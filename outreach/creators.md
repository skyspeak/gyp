# Creators

You can't pay these people. Paying for placement is the exact thing the
product refuses to do, and a sponsored gap year video would destroy the only
claim worth making. So the offer is research, not money.

That's a weaker offer, and it means most won't reply. It also means the ones
who do are worth having.

## Who to approach

**Gap year YouTubers.** Small channels, 5k to 100k subs, usually someone
documenting their own year. They need B-roll facts and comparison numbers.
Search "gap year vlog 2026", "my gap year", "AmeriCorps NCCC vlog".

**Fulbright and AmeriCorps creators.** Very small, very trusted, usually
current or former participants answering the same questions on repeat. The
closure data is genuinely useful to them.

**College counselors on TikTok and Instagram.** They post admissions advice
and get asked about deferral constantly. The $75,000 number is the hook.

**Personal finance creators.** Not gap year people at all. The angle is that
families spend $40,000 on something with a paid alternative. This is the
group most likely to make something go wide.

Skip anyone whose bio has a discount code for a gap year operator. They're
already paid by the other side and the conversation goes nowhere.

## The pitch

Short. No attachment on the first message. Lead with the number, not the ask.

**Email or DM, gap year creator**
```
Hi [name],

I maintain a free index of gap year programs. No commissions, no sponsored
listings, which is unusual in this space and the reason I can publish what
follows.

I tracked 371 programs and sorted them by whether they pay you or charge you.
231 pay. 93 charge. The priced ones run higher than most people say out loud:
a postgraduate year at a boarding school reaches $75,000, EF is around
$43,750.

I'm not pitching a sponsorship and I have no budget, which I know makes this a
worse email. If any of the data is useful for something you're making, take
it. I'll pull whatever numbers you want for a specific category and send you
the sources.

https://gyp-psi.vercel.app/?ref=[their-handle]

[your name]
```

## When someone says yes

Give them more than they asked for, fast. A category breakdown with sources
inside a day is what turns one video into a standing relationship.

Ask for exactly one thing: that they use the `?ref=` link, because it's the
only way you'll know it worked. Don't ask for approval over the script, don't
ask to see it early, and don't correct them publicly if they get a detail
slightly wrong. Send a private note instead.

## If they ask to be paid

```
I don't have a budget, and I wouldn't spend it here even if I did. The whole
value of the index is that no money moves between it and any program, which
means paid placement of any kind is off the table. Completely understand if
that's a no.
```

That answer loses most deals and keeps the thing worth promoting.
